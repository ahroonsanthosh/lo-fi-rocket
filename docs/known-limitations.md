# Known limitations

Every model limitation and validity range, per CLAUDE.md rule 8. Each entry says what the limitation is, where it applies, how big the effect is if known, and how the software tells the user.

Status: nothing physical is implemented yet. Entries below are placeholders for the sections that will exist; each implemented model adds its own entry in the same change that implements it.

## How to add an entry

| Field            | Content                                                                                         |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Model            | Function or module affected, and the equation IDs from `docs/equations.md`                      |
| Limitation       | What the model does not capture, or the range outside which it is not valid                     |
| Source statement | What the cited source says about the range, quoted or paraphrased with location                 |
| Effect           | Magnitude of the error if known, or "unknown"                                                   |
| Behaviour        | What the code does at the edge: throws `InvalidInputError`, flags the result, or documents only |
| User-facing      | Where the UI shows this (Phase 3 onwards)                                                       |

## Held-out validation data (plan 0024, D-061)

- **Coverage gap, transonic CP from Mach 1.03 to 1.2:** the only independent transonic CP data (Blue Scout, TN D-1958) end at Mach 1.03; from 1.03 to 1.2 the CP is checked only against the ARCAS data that were used to choose the fin interpolation (D-056), reported separately. Flights affected: any passing through Mach 1.03 to 1.2 (the transonic warning already applies).
- **Digitisation uncertainty against the targets:** CP uncertainties (0.2 to 0.5 % of body length) are far below the CP targets (3 to 8 %). For C_D the reading floor of ±0.01 is 4 to 10 % of the measured values of RM L8A05 (C_D 0.10 to 0.24) and 4 to 6 % of TN 2944's (0.17 to 0.26), comparable to the subsonic (7 %) and transonic (10 %) C_D targets; a C_D result within those targets on these two sets is therefore known only to about the target itself. The Blue Scout (±0.01 on 0.35 to 0.72, 1.4 to 3 %) and the RM-10 tunnel drag (±0.005 on 0.25, 2 %) are well inside. The TN 2944 drag rise carries ±0.013 between Mach 0.94 and 1.03, which moves the measured rise Mach by less than 0.01.
- **Configurations outside the model's section types:** the Blue Scout and TN D-3182 fins are single wedges with a blunt base ('square' is used, which also charges a blunt leading edge), the RM-10 and L8A05 fins circular-arc ('airfoil'). The TN D-3182 report adds a wedge lift effect that Barrowman's method does not have. Part C item 4 (DATCOM fin section drag) addresses the sections.
- **Sting-mounted base terms:** whether TN D-3182's C_A removes the base is not stated in the extracted text; its drag comparison is made against the total and the forebody value is shown beside it.

## Fin wave drag (plan 0025, D-062)

- **Low aspect ratio fins at supersonic speed:** the DATCOM supersonic wave drag (DRAG-19) is two-dimensional linear theory; it gives no three-dimensional relief, so for fins of aspect ratio about 1 to 2 the supersonic fin drag is over-predicted (RM-10 free flight: +14 % total drag at Mach 1.4 to 1.5 after the fairing, within 10 % from 1.0 to 1.3 on the chart). Flights affected: supersonic flights with thin sharp fins; the error is on the safe side for apogee (less altitude predicted).
- **Chart reading uncertainty:** figure 4.1.5.1-29 was digitised twice with disagreements up to 0.40 in C_Dw/(t/c)^(5/3) on the steep rise (0.003 in C_Dw for t/c = 0.05), and the DATCOM's own sample readings lie about 0.1 further along the abscissa than the printed curves, equivalent to a drag rise 0.03 Mach earlier for t/c = 0.06. The held-out rise placement is within 0.02 (RM-10) and 0.055 (L8A05) Mach.
- **Square (blunt plate) fins:** no DATCOM section wave drag applies; they keep Niskanen's stagnation leading-edge and base terms, which rise from Mach 0 and over-predict the Blue Scout forebody drag by 20 % on average (Part C item 4).

## Fixed-step RK4 and stiff modes (plan 0026, D-063)

- **Cause of the R2 instability:** the roll damping moment (AERO-5, C_r ω_x) acting on the small roll inertia of a slender rocket decays at C_r/I_rot, up to 330 s⁻¹ on R2 at maximum dynamic pressure; the classical RK4 step is stable only below 2.7853/λ = 0.0084 s there, so a 0.01 s step amplifies any roll rate by 2.1 per step once wind, a rail angle or round-off excites it. Fixed-step RK4 now stops with the safe step instead of returning the diverged run; the check is a linearisation at the start of each step (factor 0.9 margin), so a step accepted by it can still lose accuracy in strongly nonlinear phases (large angle of attack, the quadratic pitch damping), where the model-limit and non-finite stops apply. Flights affected: any RK4 run with a step above a few milliseconds on a light, slender, fast rocket; Dormand–Prince (the default) is unaffected.
- **Numerical damping of the pitch oscillation:** RK4 damps an oscillation of phase advance θ = h ω_n per step by 1 − √(1 − θ⁶/72 + θ⁸/576); R5 at 0.01 s loses 1 % per step (warned), which does not change apogee or velocities on the reference rockets but shortens the recorded oscillation. The stop threshold of 10 % per step is a convention.

## Supersonic body term (plan 0027, D-064)

- **Transonic fairing:** the DATCOM body charts begin at β/f_N = 0.4 (Mach 1.56 for a nose of fineness ratio 3, 2.2 for 5); between Mach 0.8 and that point the body term is a straight line in Mach between Niskanen's slender-body value and the chart's first value, the DATCOM's "fair smoothly" with no data in between. Size: on the Blue Scout the fairing moves the predicted CP 2 to 4 % of body length forward of the measured one between Mach 0.9 and 1.03 (conservative). Flights affected: every flight above Mach 0.8.
- **Shapes without a chart:** elliptical and Haack (Von Kármán, LV-Haack) noses use the ogive charts as the nearest shape, with a warning; the DATCOM offers no data for them.
- **Boattails and flares:** keep Niskanen's Mach-independent terms; the DATCOM boattail centre-of-pressure chart (figure 4.2.2.1-24, two parameters) is not implemented.

## Supersonic fin normal force and body carry-over (plan 0028, D-065)

- **Transonic fairing to Mach 1.4:** the DATCOM wing charts are for M ≥ 1.4 and its transonic wing method (4.1.3.2 B) is not implemented, so from Mach 0.8 to 1.4 the fin-set force is BAR-12's cubic between Niskanen's subsonic value and the DATCOM value at 1.4, and the carry-over share grows linearly. Size: the ARCAS C_Nα is over-predicted by up to 53 % at Mach 1.2 (the cubic overshoots); the CP stays within the transonic targets on the held-out Blue Scout (rms 3.5 % L, worst −6.1, conservative) and ARCAS (rms 4.9). Flights affected: every flight between Mach 0.8 and 1.4; the transonic and supersonic warnings apply.
- **Total C_Nα is not a v1.0 target:** with the DATCOM method it is over-predicted on ARCAS (+11 % to +39 % from Mach 1.0) and under-predicted on TN D-3182 (about −18 %); the Blue Scout transonic values are 6 % to 19 % low. C_Nα sets the pitch natural frequency and the weathercocking rate, not the stability margin.
- **RM-10 CP aft of measured:** +2.9 to +3.6 % of body length (non-conservative) at Mach 1.49 to 1.98, inside the 4 % supersonic rms target. The fins sit on a boattail (the DATCOM wing-body charts assume a cylinder at the fins) and run near a sonic leading edge, where the thickness correction of figure 4.1.3.2-60 applies.
- **Blunt leading edges near sonic:** figure 4.1.3.2-60 covers sharp-nosed sections; 'square' and 'rounded' fins get no thickness correction and a warning when the leading edge is near sonic (their fin force may be overstated, CP too far aft). 'Airfoil' fins are taken as circular-arc sections; other airfoil shapes have different leading-edge angles.
- **Chart coverage:** planforms with A_e tan Λ_LE above 6, below 0.25 with taper below 1, forward-swept leading edges or leading-edge angles above 70° fall back to Niskanen's eq. (3.49) with a warning (CP conservatively forward). Panels (b) λ = 1/5 and (d) λ = 1/3 are interpolated linearly between the digitised panels. Figure 4.3.1.2-11b is digitised to β d/c_r = 2.6 (its curves run together beyond 2.7 in the scan); outside the digitised figure 11 the carry-over is left out with a warning. The fin panels' own CP keeps Niskanen's BAR-10 (figure 4.1.4.2-26 was not digitised).
- **Afterbody threshold:** the switch between figures 4.3.1.2-11a and 11b at an afterbody of β d is this project's reading of sketch (e) (D-065), chosen so that short afterbodies take the smaller carry-over.
