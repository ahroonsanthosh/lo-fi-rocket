# Known limitations

Every model limitation and validity range, per CLAUDE.md rule 8. Each entry says what the limitation is, where it applies, how big the effect is if known, and how the software tells the user.

## Summary for v1.0

- **Validated range: Mach 0 to 2.0.** Above Mach 2 every result carries an "outside the validated range" warning. Held-out wind-tunnel and free-flight data (`docs/reports/held-out-validation.md`) give:

  | Regime     | CP rms (% body length) | CP worst | C_D rms | C_D target       |
  | ---------- | ---------------------- | -------- | ------- | ---------------- |
  | Subsonic   | 0.4                    | 0.5      | 7.1 %   | 7 %, just missed |
  | Transonic  | 4.3                    | 6.4      | 21.3 %  | 10 %, not met    |
  | Supersonic | 3.8                    | 5.1      | 16.4 %  | 8 %, not met     |

  Drag-rise Mach is within 0.05 on all sets.

- **Drag is the weakest part.** Transonic and supersonic drag, and so apogee for rockets that go past Mach 0.8, are less certain than the CP. The remaining sourced methods are listed in `docs/future-work.md`.
- **Large angles.** In the displayed mode flights continue to 180° below Mach 1 (D-070). Above 60° the methods are unverified, and the in-flight margin uses the more conservative of two models.
- **Unsourced inputs.** A few inputs have no approved source and are flagged rather than guessed: the fibreglass shear modulus for flutter, deployment-speed limits, and the stability thresholds (rules of thumb).
- **Detail.** Each model's limitation, source statement, effect and behaviour follows; later sections supersede earlier ones where they overlap, and superseded rows say so.

## How to add an entry

| Field            | Content                                                                                         |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Model            | Function or module affected, and the equation IDs from `docs/equations.md`                      |
| Limitation       | What the model does not capture, or the range outside which it is not valid                     |
| Source statement | What the cited source says about the range, quoted or paraphrased with location                 |
| Effect           | Magnitude of the error if known, or "unknown"                                                   |
| Behaviour        | What the code does at the edge: throws `InvalidInputError`, flags the result, or documents only |
| User-facing      | Where the UI shows this (Phase 3 onwards)                                                       |

## Held-out validation data (plan 0024, D-061)

- **Coverage gap, transonic CP from Mach 1.03 to 1.2:** the only independent transonic CP data (Blue Scout, TN D-1958) end at Mach 1.03; from 1.03 to 1.2 the CP is checked only against the ARCAS data that were used to choose the fin interpolation (D-056), reported separately. Flights affected: any passing through Mach 1.03 to 1.2 (the transonic warning already applies).
- **Digitisation uncertainty against the targets:** CP uncertainties (0.2 to 0.5 % of body length) are far below the CP targets (3 to 8 %). For C_D the reading floor of ±0.01 is 4 to 10 % of the measured values of RM L8A05 (C_D 0.10 to 0.24) and 4 to 6 % of TN 2944's (0.17 to 0.26), comparable to the subsonic (7 %) and transonic (10 %) C_D targets; a C_D result within those targets on these two sets is therefore known only to about the target itself. The Blue Scout (±0.01 on 0.35 to 0.72, 1.4 to 3 %) and the RM-10 tunnel drag (±0.005 on 0.25, 2 %) are well inside. The TN 2944 drag rise carries ±0.013 between Mach 0.94 and 1.03, which moves the measured rise Mach by less than 0.01.
- **Configurations outside the model's section types:** the Blue Scout and TN D-3182 fins are single wedges with a blunt base ('square' is used, which also charges a blunt leading edge), the RM-10 and L8A05 fins circular-arc ('airfoil'). The TN D-3182 report adds a wedge lift effect that Barrowman's method does not have. Part C item 4 (DATCOM fin section drag) addresses the sections.
- **Sting-mounted base terms:** whether TN D-3182's C_A removes the base is not stated in the extracted text; its drag comparison is made against the total and the forebody value is shown beside it.

## Fin wave drag (plan 0025, D-062)

- **Low aspect ratio fins at supersonic speed:** the DATCOM supersonic wave drag (DRAG-19) is two-dimensional linear theory; it gives no three-dimensional relief, so for fins of aspect ratio about 1 to 2 the supersonic fin drag is over-predicted (RM-10 free flight: +14 % total drag at Mach 1.4 to 1.5 after the fairing, within 10 % from 1.0 to 1.3 on the chart). Flights affected: supersonic flights with thin sharp fins. Apogee is under-predicted, so the rocket may fly higher than shown: the unsafe side for an altitude limit.
- **Chart reading uncertainty:** figure 4.1.5.1-29 was digitised twice with disagreements up to 0.40 in C_Dw/(t/c)^(5/3) on the steep rise (0.003 in C_Dw for t/c = 0.05), and the DATCOM's own sample readings lie about 0.1 further along the abscissa than the printed curves, equivalent to a drag rise 0.03 Mach earlier for t/c = 0.06. The held-out rise placement is within 0.02 (RM-10) and 0.055 (L8A05) Mach.
- **Square (blunt plate) fins:** no DATCOM section wave drag applies; they keep Niskanen's stagnation leading-edge and base terms, which rise from Mach 0 and over-predict the Blue Scout forebody drag by 20 % on average (Part C item 4).

## Fixed-step RK4 and stiff modes (plan 0026, D-063)

- **Cause of the R2 instability:** the roll damping moment (AERO-5, C_r ω_x) acting on the small roll inertia of a slender rocket decays at C_r/I_rot, up to 330 s⁻¹ on R2 at maximum dynamic pressure; the classical RK4 step is stable only below 2.7853/λ = 0.0084 s there, so a 0.01 s step amplifies any roll rate by 2.1 per step once wind, a rail angle or round-off excites it. Fixed-step RK4 now stops with the safe step instead of returning the diverged run; the check is a linearisation at the start of each step (factor 0.9 margin), so a step accepted by it can still lose accuracy in strongly nonlinear phases (large angle of attack, the quadratic pitch damping), where the model-limit and non-finite stops apply. Flights affected: any RK4 run with a step above a few milliseconds on a light, slender, fast rocket; Dormand–Prince (the default) is unaffected.
- **Numerical damping of the pitch oscillation:** RK4 damps an oscillation of phase advance θ = h ω_n per step by 1 − √(1 − θ⁶/72 + θ⁸/576); R5 at 0.01 s loses 1 % per step (warned), which does not change apogee or velocities on the reference rockets but shortens the recorded oscillation. The stop threshold of 10 % per step is a convention.

## Supersonic body term (plan 0027, D-064)

- **Transonic fairing:** the DATCOM body charts begin at β/f_N = 0.4 (Mach 1.56 for a nose of fineness ratio 3, 2.2 for 5); between Mach 0.8 and that point the body term is a straight line in Mach between Niskanen's slender-body value and the chart's first value, the DATCOM's "fair smoothly" with no data in between. Size: on the Blue Scout the fairing moves the predicted CP 2 to 4 % of body length forward of the measured one between Mach 0.9 and 1.03 (conservative). Flights affected: every flight above Mach 0.8.
- **Shapes without a chart:** elliptical and Haack (Von Kármán, LV-Haack) noses use the ogive charts as the nearest shape, with a warning; the DATCOM offers no data for them.
- **Boattails and flares:** keep Niskanen's Mach-independent terms; the DATCOM boattail centre-of-pressure chart (figure 4.2.2.1-24, two parameters) is not implemented.

## Supersonic fin normal force and body carry-over (plan 0028, D-065)

- **Transonic fairing to Mach 1.4:** the DATCOM wing charts are for M ≥ 1.4 and its transonic wing method (4.1.3.2 B) is not implemented, so from Mach 0.8 to 1.4 the fin-set force is BAR-12's cubic between Niskanen's subsonic value and the DATCOM value at 1.4, and the carry-over share grows linearly. Size: the ARCAS C_Nα is over-predicted by up to 53 % at Mach 1.2 (the cubic overshoots); the CP stays within the transonic targets on the held-out Blue Scout (rms 3.5 % L, worst −6.1, conservative) and ARCAS (rms 4.9). Flights affected: every flight between Mach 0.8 and 1.4; the transonic and supersonic warnings apply.
- **Total C_Nα is not a v1.0 target:** with the DATCOM method it is over-predicted on ARCAS (+11 % to +39 % from Mach 1.0) and under-predicted on TN D-3182 (about −18 %); the Blue Scout transonic values are 6 % to 19 % low. C_Nα sets the pitch natural frequency and the weathercocking rate, not the stability margin.
- **RM-10 CP aft of measured:** +2.9 to +3.6 % of body length (non-conservative) at Mach 1.49 to 1.98, inside the 4 % supersonic rms target. The fins sit on a boattail (the DATCOM wing-body charts assume a cylinder at the fins) and run near a sonic leading edge, where the thickness correction of figure 4.1.3.2-60 applies.
- **Blunt leading edges near sonic:** figure 4.1.3.2-60 covers sharp-nosed sections; 'square' and 'rounded' fins get no thickness correction and a warning when the leading edge is near sonic (their fin force may be overstated, CP too far aft). 'Airfoil' fins are taken as circular-arc sections; other airfoil shapes have different leading-edge angles.
- **Chart coverage:** planforms with A_e tan Λ_LE above 6, below 0.25 with taper below 1, forward-swept leading edges or leading-edge angles above 70° fall back to Niskanen's eq. (3.49) with a warning (CP conservatively forward). Panels (b) λ = 1/5 and (d) λ = 1/3 are interpolated linearly between the digitised panels. Figure 4.3.1.2-11b is digitised to β d/c_r = 2.6 (its curves run together beyond 2.7 in the scan); outside the digitised figure 11 the carry-over is left out with a warning. The fin panels' own CP keeps Niskanen's BAR-10 (figure 4.1.4.2-26 was not digitised).
- **Afterbody threshold:** the switch between figures 4.3.1.2-11a and 11b at an afterbody of β d is this project's reading of sketch (e) (D-065), chosen so that short afterbodies take the smaller carry-over.

## Fin sections (plan 0029, D-066)

- **Sharp wedges under-predicted supersonically:** with the 'wedge' and 'double-wedge' sections the supersonic drag of the TN D-3182 model (−21 % mean), TM X-1661 (−17 %) and the ARCAS forebody (−19 %) is low, where circular-arc fins (RM-10) are within 5 %. Linear-theory wave drag of thick wedges and the base drag of a blunt fin trailing edge (Niskanen's body base formula applied to the edge) are the likely causes; no approved method for either was found. Flights affected: supersonic flights with wedge fins. Apogee is over-predicted, so the rocket may fly lower than shown: the safe side for an altitude limit, but an ejection delay chosen from the predicted coast may fire after apogee.
- **Transonic wave drag of non-biconvex sections:** figure 4.1.5.1-29 is drawn for one section family; wedges take it in the ratio of their supersonic K. The ARCAS forebody (double wedge) is 28 % low in the transonic band and the Blue Scout (wedge) 15 % to 24 % low from Mach 0.9 to 1.03.
- **Flat-faced (square) leading edges:** the DATCOM has no method; Niskanen's stagnation term remains, unvalidated on independent data.
- **Rounded leading edges below Mach 0.5:** the DATCOM correlation was substantiated from Mach 0.5; below it the term is small (under 0.14 of the edge frontal area) and is used as written, with a warning.
- **Wedge geometry:** the model's wedge has a constant thickness t over the span with faces rising t/(2c) along each chord; real wedges with a fixed included angle are thicker at the root.

## In-flight stability (plan 0030, D-067)

- **Near apogee:** the coast minimum is usually near apogee, where the angle of attack grows as the airspeed falls (13° to 20° on the reference rockets) and body lift moves the CP forward. The dynamic pressure there is small, so a warning caused by it matters less than one early in the flight; the warning names the time, Mach number and angle of attack so the user can tell. Flights affected: every flight with wind or an angled rail.
- **Angle-of-attack model:** the CP at angle of attack uses Niskanen's body lift (eq. 3.26) and linear fins; above about 10° the fin force is outside Barrowman's small-angle assumption (Part C item 8 extends it).

## Fin flutter (plan 0031, D-068)

- **Empirical criterion:** TN 4197's formula was "adjusted until a reasonable coherence" with the data of its figure 3, and the boundary between safe and unsafe wings is a shaded band; a ratio V_f/V near 1 is uncertain either way (R2 is at 1.10). It covers bending-torsion flutter of thin solid panels; it does not cover fin-can or root-joint flexibility, composite lay-ups (whose effective G_E must be measured, eq. 12), or stall flutter. Flights affected: every flight; the margin is a screening value, not a guarantee.
- **Materials:** only wood, magnesium, aluminium, titanium and steel have sourced moduli (TN 4197 figure 3, read at about ±5 %); plywood is taken as wood; fibreglass, carbon and balsa need a user-entered value, without which the flight carries a "not checked" note.
- **Constant thickness ratio:** the formula assumes constant t/c; model rocket fins have constant thickness, so t/c is evaluated on the mid-span chord.

## Body base drag (plan 0032, D-069)

- **Fins and stings at the base:** the DATCOM correlation is for an isolated body and notes that fins and stings change the base pressure; the sting-mounted Blue Scout's measured base force is 58 % to 68 % above it (subsonic and transonic). Flights affected: rockets with large blunt fin trailing edges at the base; their drag may be under-predicted (apogee over-predicted).
- **Supersonic base drag:** above Mach 1.295 the DATCOM guide ends; the base drag is faired to Niskanen's eq. (3.94) at Mach 1.5 and follows it above. The DATCOM supersonic base-pressure charts (figures 4.2.3.1-50, -55 and -60) are not implemented. The RM-10 flight drag is 7.6 % low on average with the fairing.
- **Guide-curve range:** a subsonic base drag above 0.12 or below −0.19 (on the base area) is outside figure 4.2.3.1-24; eq. (3.94) is used with a warning.
- **Boat tails beside the DATCOM base (D-105):** a boat tail's pressure drag is Niskanen's eq. (3.88), a fraction of his base drag coefficient (3.94), which is higher than the DATCOM base drag. In the displayed mode a short boat tail or tail cone can therefore be charged more pressure drag than the base drag it removes (R1 with an 80 mm ogive tail at Mach 0.3: +0.092 against −0.060). The alternative, a fraction of the DATCOM's own blunt-base drag, was tried (plan 0069). It moved R2's apogee by 0.66 % but doubled the subsonic error of the ARCAS forebody drag (−8 % to −20 % rms), the only measured case in `reference/` with a boat tail short enough (γ < 3) for eq. (3.88) to charge, so it was not adopted. Flights affected: rockets with a boat tail or tail cone, in the displayed mode; apogee may be slightly under-predicted (the unsafe side for an altitude limit).

## Large angles of attack (plan 0033, D-070)

- **Approximate above 60°.** The DATCOM's body method has no test verification above 60° at subsonic speeds (p. 4.2.1.2-6). The fin method is an empirical correlation. Tumbling and backward flight are therefore approximate, and a warning says so.
- **Reverse flow is an extension.** Fins at 90 to 180° are evaluated as the reversed wing (α′ = 180° − α). The DATCOM uses that idea in its own discussion (p. 4.1.3.3-3) but gives no method for it. The reverse-flow body term is substantiated only for constant cross-section bodies, and a warning is given when the body has a transition.
- **Mach range.** The fin charts stop at Mach 0.6. They are read at 0.6 up to Mach 1, with a warning beyond the stall. Above Mach 1 the small-angle fin model applies up to 90°. Beyond 90° the same force is applied to the reversed fin (D-103). No sourced method covers this, so a flight that tumbles above Mach 1 lists it as a major approximation: the tumbling motion, the path and the landing point are uncertain.
- **Sections.** Single-wedge fins are approximated: maximum thickness at the trailing edge lies outside figures 4.1.3.4-23a and -23b, and the 23b family (maximum thickness at 35 to 50 %), the nearer, is used. This is listed in the result (D-102). For typical fins the two families give the same stall angle and maximum lift within 0.4 %, and 8 % apart for a strongly swept one. Square-edged fins have Δy = 0, which lies below the drawn curves of figures 4.1.3.4-23a (right family) and 4.1.4.3-21a; the nearest curve is used, with a warning beyond the stall.
- **Aspect ratio.** Fins whose exposed pair has A ≥ 4/((C₁ + 1) cos Λ_LE) are high-aspect-ratio in the DATCOM's sense. The DATCOM's method for them needs section maximum-lift data, which a flat plate does not have. The low-aspect-ratio method is used beyond its range instead, its charts held at their ends, and this is listed in the result (D-102). The size of the error is unknown: no measured large-angle data for such fins are in reference/.
- **Not included.** The body-vortex interference of DATCOM 4.3.1.3, which the DATCOM says "may become sizable" for missiles, is not included. Neither is Jorgensen's axial force: beyond 90° the axial drag is Niskanen's scaling mirrored (D-033).
- **Moderate angles.** The two normal-force models differ in the in-flight CP at 10 to 20° by up to 0.8 calibers. Measured Blue Scout data show a larger forward CP shift than either model. The reported in-flight margin uses the more forward CP (D-070).
- **Chart reading.** In figure 4.1.4.3-23(a) the curves are hidden under the gridline within about ±0.3 of stability index 0. Δ₃ there is interpolated and uncertain by up to about ±0.2 root chords.

## Pod sets (plan 0070, D-106)

- **Each pod on its own.** A pod set's parts are counted once per pod, in their mass, normal force, drag and damping, as OpenRocket does. The flow between the pods and the body (interference, shielding, the pods' effect on the body's base pressure) is not modelled by either, and no approved source gives it. Each flight result lists it as a moderate approximation.
- **One or two pods.** A single pod's CG sits off the axis, which tilts a real flight; the model keeps it on the axis (listed). Two pods give different pitch and yaw inertias; the axisymmetric model uses their mean (listed, minor). OpenRocket uses the smaller one for both.
- **Roll.** A pod's fins damp roll about their own pod's axis; about the rocket's axis their damping is larger.
- **Reverse flow.** In the displayed mode, beyond 90° the pods have no body potential term of their own.
- **Not yet in files.** Plan 0070 step 3 brings pod sets into the design model and the `.ork` reader; until then a pod set in a file is stood in or refused as before.

## Security (plan 0061 step 1, D-090)

- **Size limits.** Files over 64 MiB, `.ork` archives whose design entry unzips past 256 MiB, and `.ork` designs whose part outside the stored simulations is over 16 MiB are refused with a message. No real design or motor file is near these limits.
- **Document type declarations.** An XML file with a `<!DOCTYPE` or `<!ENTITY` declaration is refused, even inside a comment. OpenRocket and RockSim never write one.
- **Framing.** The site is served by GitHub Pages, which cannot send headers, and a Content-Security-Policy in a meta tag cannot carry `frame-ancestors`. Another site can therefore show the app in a frame. The app has no account, password or key that a framing site could misuse; this must be revisited before anything secret is ever entered in the page.
- **Development server.** `vite` in development runs without the policy, because it injects inline scripts for hot reloading. The built site (`vite build`, `vite preview`, GitHub Pages) has it.

## The MCP server (plan 0062, D-092)

- **Designs are kept only while the connection is open.** Nothing is written to disk; `export_design` returns the file for the user to save.
- **Motors.** The library holds the eight bundled motors, the motor files imported in the session and the ThrustCurve.org motors downloaded in it (D-093); downloads are not kept after the connection closes.
- **Monte Carlo** runs at most 200 flights per call (about a minute; D-093).
- **Defaults.** Values Claude does not give take the app's editing defaults and are listed as approximations; their impact ratings are rules of this app (major for the outline, moderate for masses, minor for lugs and buttons), not measured errors.
- **Launch settings not given** (site, pad temperature and pressure, rail, wind) are reported with every result but do not lower the confidence rating, as in the app.
- **Unsigned extension.** The Claude Desktop extension is not code-signed (D-094); its SHA-256 is published beside it. There is no release channel other than the site, which updates on every merge.

## Multi-stage flight (plan 0064, D-096)

- **Separation is instantaneous**, with no separation impulse; the separated stages' own flight (apogee, descent, landing) is not simulated (a version-1 non-goal, D-091).
- **Interstage aerodynamics** just before and after separation are not represented: each configuration is its attached parts alone.
- **Imports and editing.** Design files hold stages (schema 2, D-097). Staged `.ork` files import (D-098), and stages are edited in the MCP server (D-099) and the app (D-100). The app's motor view shows one mount per stage; a second motor in a stage (an air start) comes from a file or the MCP server.
- **A stage switched off between stages that fly** (an `.ork` configuration) has its gap closed: the stages below move up, since the model needs one continuous body. OpenRocket keeps the gap, with its CG further aft and extra drag (listed as major).
- **Stability of a staged design.** The design's CP and CG figures are the whole stack's. Each configuration's margin is listed: its bottom stage's motors loaded, then spent, with the motors above loaded. The burnout gauge shows the top stage alone. The moments between these are covered by the flight's in-flight margin check (plan 0030).
- **Several motors in the stage below.** An ignition at the burnout or ejection charge of the stage below follows that stage's first motor (`motorMount` first, then the additional mounts in order). OpenRocket's "first burnout of previous stage" is the earliest of them.
