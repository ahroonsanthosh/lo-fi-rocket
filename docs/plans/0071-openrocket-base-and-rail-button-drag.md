# Plan 0071: OpenRocket's base drag rule and rail button drag, re-measured (the comparison mode)

## 1. What was found

The pod motor probe of plan 0070 step 4 (`PodProbe --motors`, `reference/rockets/pod-probe/motors.txt`) flew R1's airframe with two pods and Estes D12 motors in three ways: in the core and both pods, in the pods only, and in the core only. Each gave the same drag coefficient at the same Mach number, thrusting or coasting. That coefficient also equalled OpenRocket's static calculation at that Mach number, whose base term is the whole base: 0.1213 at Mach 0.1, eq. (3.94) on the full base area.

The reference rockets show the same (`DragProbe`, `reference/rockets/drag-probe/values.txt` parts 1 and 2):

- **Base term.** With the motors in place, R1, R3, R4 and R5 have a base term of 0.1213 at Mach 0.1: the whole base.
- **Time series.** The drag coefficient in `reference/rockets/timeseries/` is the static one to 0.0004, thrusting and coasting.
- **Force.** The drag force is ½ρV²A·C_D with that coefficient, within 0.04 % at every sample checked.

So OpenRocket 24.12 never removes the motor's area from the base. D-035 had concluded that it always does, and the comparison mode removed it (`'always'`, and one motor of a cluster, D-086).

## 2. What the rule was offsetting

OpenRocket's rail button or launch lug drag was found by removing those parts and computing again, at Mach 0.1:

| Rocket | Part           | OpenRocket | Ours (Niskanen eq. 3.95 / p. 52) | Ratio |
| ------ | -------------- | ---------- | -------------------------------- | ----- |
| R1     | launch lug     | 0.00755    | 0.01227                          | 1.6   |
| R2     | 2 rail buttons | 0.01158    | 0.07441                          | 6.4   |
| R3     | 2 rail buttons | 0.03569    | 0.21526                          | 6.0   |
| R4     | 2 rail buttons | 0.00484    | 0.04981                          | 10.3  |
| R5     | 2 rail buttons | 0.01549    | 0.13562                          | 8.8   |

Removing the motor's area lowered our drag by about as much as our rail buttons added. With the full base and nothing else changed, the comparison mode's apogees would have been 6 to 10 % low on R2, R3 and R5.

## 3. Decision (the owner, 2026-10-14: option (c))

The comparison mode follows OpenRocket in both respects:

- **Base.** It keeps the whole base, `baseDragMotorRule: 'never'`.
- **Rail buttons.** It computes them as OpenRocket does, measured as a black box (DRAG-23).

The displayed mode is unchanged: Niskanen's rail pins (DRAG-12) and his base rule, removing the thrusting motors' area.

## 4. OpenRocket's rail button drag, measured (`ProtuberanceProbe`, `reference/rockets/drag-probe/`)

- **Frontal area.** One button's C_D·A is a coefficient K times its frontal area A = OD (base + flange) + ID (height − base − flange). Each of these is exactly linear in the sweeps, so their weights are equal: the button is not integrated through a velocity profile.
- **What K depends on.** K depends on the Mach number and on η = H/δ only. Here δ = 0.37 x Re_x^−0.2, the turbulent flat-plate boundary layer thickness at the button's front x from the nose tip, and Re_x = V x/ν.
  - Eight positions at the same η agree within 0.05 % (`rail-button-collapse.csv`).
  - A colder or warmer atmosphere moves K by the Reynolds number's change (`rail-button-temperature.csv`).
- **What K does not depend on.** Not the rocket's length, the body radius, the fins or the other buttons. Each of several buttons counts at its own position.
- **The table.** K was tabulated over Mach 0.01 to 5 (43 rows) and η 0.1 to 100 (about 60 points per row). It is interpolated log-log in η and linearly in Mach (`openrocket-rail-button-table.ts`, generated by `reference/digitised/build-openrocket-rail-button-table.py`).
  - **Held-out buttons.** On 80 random buttons held out of the table (size, position, count, spacing, body, Mach) it gives OpenRocket's figure within 0.82 % (rms 0.23 %).
  - **Sweeps.** On the sweeps (position, Mach 0.05 to 4, every dimension, count, body) it is within 1 %.
- **Beyond the table.** Below the table K ∝ η², the table's own slope there (1.97 to 2.07); above it, the last value is held.

## 6. A difference that remains: nose cone pressure drag below Mach 1

`staged-hpr`'s sustainer peaks at Mach 0.86 behind a tangent ogive. Its parts against OpenRocket (`values.txt` part 4) agree, except the nose:

| Part            | OpenRocket at Mach 0.85 | Ours                                               |
| --------------- | ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Fins            | 3 × 0.06926 = 0.2078    | 0.2078                                             |
| Base            | 0.2139                  | 0.2139                                             |
| Friction        | 0.2751                  | 0.2794 (+1.6 %)                                    |
| Nose (pressure) | 0.0024                  | 0.0909 (Niskanen eq. (3.87) with the (B.8) factor) |

`NoseProbe` (`values.txt` part 5) shows how OpenRocket 24.12 computes nose pressure drag against Mach:

- **Cones.** Niskanen's method: eq. (3.86) at low speed, rising to sin ε at Mach 1. This matches ours.
- **Tangent ogives (κ = 1).** The low-speed value is held flat to Mach 1. There is a narrow bump at Mach 1.05 to 1.2, and then almost nothing: 0.0005 to 0.002 from Mach 1.3 to 3.
  - Niskanen's method gives 0.82 × the cone's value at the same fineness, about 0.1 at Mach 2 for f = 3.
  - A pointed nose has wave drag at supersonic speed, so near-zero is not physical.
- **Ellipsoid, power, parabolic and Haack noses.** OpenRocket gives zero (or near zero for the ellipsoid) to Mach 0.9, then its own curve. Ours follows Niskanen Appendix B.3 with Stoney's measured data (TR R-100, D-055).

The comparison mode does not copy this. It looks like an OpenRocket error, not a method: it departs from OpenRocket's own documentation and from physics. Which is closer to reality is being checked against measured data, the ARCAS ogive-nose models at Mach 2.01 among them (validation campaign, task 18). `staged-hpr`'s maximum velocity check carries the explanation, at 4.5 %.

## 7. Also recorded, not changed here

- **Launch lugs.** OpenRocket's lug drag does not depend on position. It is a term proportional to the lug's length plus a constant, not the stagnation lug of eq. (3.95): R1's lug is 0.00755 against our 0.01227. Both modes keep eq. (3.95).
- **Skin friction.** Ours is about 1.4 % above OpenRocket's on every reference rocket. This is the next term to compare (task 18).
- **Viscosity.** OpenRocket's viscosity at 293.15 K is 1.5256e-5 m²/s against 1.5061e-5 by the 1976 Standard Atmosphere (1.3 %). This moves the rail button drag by about 0.4 % and the friction slightly.
