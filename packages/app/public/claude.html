Use Lo-Fi Rocket with Claude

Describe your rocket to Claude, with its specification, a kit's parts list, a photo, or an OpenRocket or RASAero II file. Claude turns it into a design and the simulator flies it, with the same sourced physics as the rest of this site. Everything you did not give, and every figure Claude had to estimate, is listed with the results, together with a confidence rating, so you can correct the ones that matter.

On this site, with nothing to download

  1. In the simulator, press Build with Claude in the top bar and copy the prompt.
  2. Paste it into any Claude app (claude.ai, the desktop or phone app, Claude Code), then describe your rocket or attach a photo, a parts list or a file.
  3. Paste Claude's answer back into the box. The site checks it, builds the design and flies it at once. If something in the answer does not fit, the box says what, in a sentence you can give back to Claude.

What it costs, and what it can reach

  • Claude runs on your own Claude plan, in your own Claude app. This project has no account, no key and no server, and charges nothing.
  • Claude's answer, and any file you paste or drop, is read in your browser. Nothing is uploaded.
  • The only network use is the ThrustCurve.org motor catalogue, and only when the design names a motor that is not in the library. It only reads from it.

Or let Claude drive the simulator

With the download, Claude calls the simulator itself: it builds, changes one part, flies again and runs the Monte Carlo spread, without copying and pasting. It runs on your own computer, reads and writes no files, and runs no commands. Designs come in as content you give Claude, and leave as a file or a link Claude gives back to you.

Claude Desktop

  1. Download lo-fi-rocket.mcpb, the extension.
  2. Open the file with Claude Desktop, or install it from Claude Desktop's settings, under Extensions. Claude Desktop runs it with its own Node.js.
  3. Ask, for example: "Model a 38 mm minimum-diameter rocket, 1.2 m long, with an AeroTech H128W, and tell me its stability and apogee."

Claude Code

  1. You need Node.js 18 or later.
  2. Download lo-fi-rocket-mcp.mjs, the server, to a folder you keep.
  3. Add it to Claude Code, with the full path to the file: 
    claude mcp add --transport stdio lo-fi-rocket -- node /full/path/to/lo-fi-rocket-mcp.mjs

Check the download

The SHA-256 of each file is in SHA256SUMS. Compare it with shasum -a 256 lo-fi-rocket-mcp.mjs (macOS, Linux) or Get-FileHash lo-fi-rocket-mcp.mjs (Windows PowerShell).

What to expect

  • Give what you know: dimensions, materials or weights, the motor as you would write it ("D12-5", "G80-7T"), the parachute, and the launch site, rail and wind. Values you do not give take a default and are marked "not given" in the results; give any of them later and Claude flies it again.
  • If your rocket is built, give its weighed mass and balance point with the motor out. They correct the computed figures.
  • Results are predictions with a stated confidence, not guarantees. Stability, rail exit speed and deployment speed still need your judgement, and your club's or the manufacturer's rules.
  • With the download, designs last only while Claude is connected. Ask Claude for a link to the design: it opens here with one click, and this site keeps it. Claude can also export the design as a file, which this site opens with File > Open.
