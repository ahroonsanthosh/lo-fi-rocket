Use Lo-Fi Rocket with Claude

Paste your rocket's specification, a kit's parts list, a photo, or an OpenRocket or RASAero II file into the chat. Claude builds the design from what you gave and flies it at once, without a questionnaire first. The simulator computes the stability, the flight, the landing and the Monte Carlo spread with the same sourced physics as this site. Everything you did not give, and every figure Claude had to estimate, is listed with the results, together with a confidence rating, so you can correct the ones that matter.

What it costs, and what it can reach

  • It runs on your own computer and uses your own Claude plan. Nothing is hosted, and there is no account and no charge from this project.
  • The simulator reads and writes no files and runs no commands. Designs come in as content you give Claude, and leave as a file Claude gives back to you.
  • Its only network use is the ThrustCurve.org motor catalogue, and only when you name or ask for a motor that is not in the library. It only reads from it.

Claude Desktop

  1. Download lo-fi-rocket.mcpb, the extension.
  2. Open the file with Claude Desktop, or install it from Claude Desktop's settings, under Extensions. Claude Desktop runs it with its own Node.js.
  3. Ask, for example: "Model a 38 mm minimum-diameter rocket, 1.2 m long, with an AeroTech H128W, and tell me its stability and apogee."

Claude Code

  1. You need Node.js 18 or later.
  2. Download lo-fi-rocket-mcp.mjs, the server, to a folder you keep.
  3. Add it to Claude Code, with the full path to the file: 
    claude mcp add --transport stdio lo-fi-rocket -- node /full/path/to/lo-fi-rocket-mcp.mjs

Check the download

The SHA-256 of each file is in SHA256SUMS. Compare it with shasum -a 256 lo-fi-rocket-mcp.mjs (macOS, Linux) or Get-FileHash lo-fi-rocket-mcp.mjs (Windows PowerShell).

What to expect

  • Give what you know: dimensions, materials or weights, the motor as you would write it ("D12-5", "G80-7T"), the parachute, and the launch site, rail and wind. Values you do not give take a default and are marked "not given" in the results; give any of them later and Claude flies it again.
  • If your rocket is built, give its weighed mass and balance point with the motor out. They correct the computed figures.
  • Results are predictions with a stated confidence, not guarantees. Stability, rail exit speed and deployment speed still need your judgement, and your club's or the manufacturer's rules.
  • Designs last only while Claude is connected. Ask Claude for a link to the design: it opens here with one click, and this site keeps it. Claude can also export the design as a file, which this site opens with File > Open.
